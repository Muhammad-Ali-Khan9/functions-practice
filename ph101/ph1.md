### Q1. Resultant vs. Equibrant

### Q2. Maximum Vector Magnitude

### Q6. Single Force Gravitational Balance (5 Marks)
A student hangs a mass of $m = 65.0\text{ g}$ from a string passing over a pulley locked at an angle of $270.0^\circ$ on the force table rim. 
1. Compute the magnitude of the downward gravitational force ($\vec{F}_g$) in Newtons. *(Take $g = 9.80\text{ m/s}^2$)*
2. Specify the exact magnitude and angle direction required for the equilibrant force ($\vec{E}$) to balance this single mass.

### Q7. Two Perpendicular Forces (5 Marks)
Two strings exert perpendicular pulling forces on a central ring:
* $\vec{F}_x = 0.60\text{ N}$ pulling directly along the positive x-axis ($0^\circ$).
* $\vec{F}_y = 0.80\text{ N}$ pulling directly along the positive y-axis ($90^\circ$).

Calculate the total **magnitude** and the **coordinate angle ($\theta$)** of the single resultant force vector ($\vec{R}$) produced by these two components.

### Q8. Three-Force Equilibrium Challenge (10 Marks)
A central ring is balanced under three concurrent forces. Two vectors are defined by the positions of the hanging masses on the pulleys:
* $\vec{F}_1 = 0.30\text{ N}$ at $\theta_1 = 45.0^\circ$
* $\vec{F}_2 = 0.50\text{ N}$ at $\theta_2 = 120.0^\circ$

Find the required **magnitude** and **angle ($\theta_3$)** of the third force ($\vec{F}_3$) needed to maintain static equilibrium ($\sum\vec{F} = 0$).

# ANSWER KEY & SOLUTIONS MANUAL

## Section A: Multiple-Choice Solutions
* **Q1: C** — The equilibrant force balances the system by counteracting the net resultant pull, meaning it must share the same magnitude but line up in the exact opposite direction ($180^\circ$ shift).
* **Q2: D** — The maximum magnitude occurs when both vectors point in the same direction ($0^\circ$ between them), allowing direct scalar addition: $5.0\text{ N} + 3.0\text{ N} = 8.0\text{ N}$.
* **Q3: A** — The vertical component is found using sine: $F_y = F \cdot \sin(\theta) = 10.0\text{ N} \cdot \sin(30^\circ) = 10.0 \cdot 0.5 = 5.0\text{ N}$.
* **Q4: B** — Real-world pulleys contain small amounts of internal mechanical friction. Tapping shakes the system past this frictional resistance threshold to reveal the true equilibrium position.
* **Q5: C** — According to Newton's First Law, an object at rest must experience zero net acceleration, which demands that the vector sum of all concurrent forces equals zero ($\sum\vec{F} = 0$).

## Section B: Step-by-Step Computational Solutions

### Solution to Q6:
1. **Convert mass to kilograms:**
   $$m = 65.0\text{ g} = 0.0650\text{ kg}$$
2. **Calculate Gravitational Force:**
   $$F_g = m \cdot g = 0.0650\text{ kg} \times 9.80\text{ m/s}^2 = \mathbf{0.637\text{ N}}$$
3. **Determine Equibrant Force ($\vec{E}$):**
   To balance a force pointing down at $270^\circ$, the equilibrant must pull in the opposite direction.
   * **Magnitude:** $\mathbf{0.637\text{ N}}$
   * **Direction Angle:** $270.0^\circ - 180.0^\circ = \mathbf{90.0^\circ}$

### Solution to Q7:
1. **Calculate Magnitude of Resultant ($\vec{R}$):**
   Using the Pythagorean theorem for orthogonal vectors:
   $$R = \sqrt{(F_x)^2 + (F_y)^2} = \sqrt{(0.60)^2 + (0.80)^2} = \sqrt{0.36 + 0.64} = \sqrt{1.00} = \mathbf{1.00\text{ N}}$$
2. **Calculate Direction Angle ($\theta$):**
   $$\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{F_y}{F_x}\right) = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{0.80}{0.60}\right) = \tan^{-1}(1.3333) \approx \mathbf{53.1^\circ}$$
   *(Since both components are positive, the vector lies in Quadrant I).*

### Solution to Q8:
For the system to be in static equilibrium, the sum of all components must equal zero:
$$F_{3x} = -(F_{1x} + F_{2x}) \quad \text{and} \quad F_{3y} = -(F_{1y} + F_{2y})$$

1. **Resolve $\vec{F}_1$ and $\vec{F}_2$ into components:**
   * $F_{1x} = 0.30 \cdot \cos(45^\circ) = 0.30 \cdot 0.7071 = 0.2121\text{ N}$
   * $F_{1y} = 0.30 \cdot \sin(45^\circ) = 0.30 \cdot 0.7071 = 0.2121\text{ N}$
   * $F_{2x} = 0.50 \cdot \cos(120^\circ) = 0.50 \cdot (-0.5000) = -0.2500\text{ N}$
   * $F_{2y} = 0.50 \cdot \sin(120^\circ) = 0.50 \cdot 0.8660 = 0.4330\text{ N}$

2. **Calculate the components of $\vec{F}_3$:**
   * $F_{3x} = -(0.2121 + (-0.2500)) = -(-0.0379) = \mathbf{+0.0379\text{ N}}$
   * $F_{3y} = -(0.2121 + 0.4330) = -(0.6451) = \mathbf{-0.6451\text{ N}}$

3. **Calculate the final magnitude of $\vec{F}_3$:**
   $$F_3 = \sqrt{(F_{3x})^2 + (F_{3y})^2} = \sqrt{(0.0379)^2 + (-0.6451)^2} = \sqrt{0.001436 + 0.416154} \approx \mathbf{0.646\text{ N}}$$

4. **Calculate the direction angle ($\theta_3$):**
   Find the reference angle in the coordinate system:
   $$\theta_{\text{ref}} = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{|F_{3y}|}{|F_{3x}|}\right) = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{0.6451}{0.0379}\right) = \tan^{-1}(17.021) \approx 86.6^\circ$$
   Because $F_{3x}$ is positive $(+)$ and $F_{3y}$ is negative $(-)$, the force vector lies in **Quadrant IV**:
   $$\theta_3 = 360.0^\circ - 86.6^\circ = \mathbf{273.4^\circ}$$
